How this compares to industry planning software

Advisory firms license planning platforms such as MoneyGuidePro, eMoney and RightCapital to run these conversations with clients. This lab rebuilds the core methods behind them from scratch, to show I understand how the numbers work, not just how to read the output.

Methods

Portfolio math. Expected return is the weighted average of each asset class; risk comes from a correlation matrix converted to covariance (w′Σw). Example ETFs: VTI, VXUS, BND and SCHP. A 1% advisory fee is deducted.

Projection. Contributions grow 3% a year until retirement. Withdrawals cover spending minus Social Security and rise with 2.5% inflation.

Simulation. Each of 1,000 runs draws a random return every year from the portfolio's return and volatility. Probability of success is the share of runs where money lasts to age 92. The bad-luck case uses an approximate 10th-percentile return: expected return − 1.28 × volatility ÷ √30.

Accounts. The same pre-tax income goes into each account. Roth and taxable contributions are taxed first; Traditional and 401(k) withdrawals are taxed at the retirement rate; taxable accounts lose 0.5% a year to tax drag and pay 15% on gains.

Practice ROI. New clients = prospects × 12 × conversion. Clients and insured clients carry forward at the retention rate. Revenue = advisory fee on average assets + annuity commissions + life insurance first-year and renewal commissions. Client lifetime value is the NPV of 10 years of expected cash flow at an 8% discount rate.

Business. Customers × price, less 25% cost of service and fixed costs. Each simulated future has its own growth and churn rate plus month-to-month noise.
